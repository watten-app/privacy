2. Overview

Watten is a card game. Playing against the computer and taking turns on one device run entirely on your device, without an account and without a network. There is also an online mode: it is optional, and the app connects to the online service only if you open it and use an online game (see section 4). For this the app has the permission to use the internet and, for the optional premium subscription, the Google Play permission for in-app purchases; the only permission you can grant is showing notifications, which Android asks for only if you turn them on. Android libraries also add a purely internal permission that concerns only the app itself and gives no access to data or devices. No advertising, analytics or tracking services are included.

3. Data in the app

The app stores the following information only locally, in the app’s private storage on your device:

  • your settings (language, rules, table colour, sounds, volume, colour-blind symbols),
  • player names, if you enter any,
  • the state of a game in progress, so you can resume it,
  • statistics about your games against the computer,
  • if you use the online mode: the address of the online service, your email address, and the account ID and device key of your online account.

With the exception of the online mode details (section 4), this data is not sent to the provider or to third parties. You can delete it at any time by clearing the app’s data in Android settings or by uninstalling the app. Android’s automatic backup is switched off for the app, so the data is not included in a backup of your Google account.

4. Online mode (optional)

In the online mode you play with other people on different devices. For this I run an online service. It runs at Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA) using Pages, Workers and D1. To send the confirmation codes by email I use Brevo (Sendinblue SAS, 106 boulevard Haussmann, 75008 Paris, France). Both are processors; for the optional push notifications and for checking purchases Google is added (see below). If you do not use the online mode, the app does not connect to this service.

Account with an email address. For the online mode you sign in with your email address. You enter it in the app and receive a six-digit code by email, which you confirm in the app. With a new address the service creates an account; with a known address you sign in to your existing account, also on a different device, for example after reinstalling or on a new phone. I need the address to send you the code, to find your account again and to delete it on request. It is never shown to other players. The service also assigns a random account ID, a random key for each device (stored on the server only as a check value) and a random player name, for example “Flinker-Fuchs-417”. You can roll a new random name or choose your own display name (3 to 20 characters); it is shown to your fellow players, so please do not use a real name you do not want to share, and nothing offensive. There is no free text such as a chat. Please do not enter an address that does not belong to you. The address is required for an online account; without it the online mode cannot be used, and the modes on your device are not affected.

Saved game partners and invitations. Anyone who joins a game with a code and the creator of that game are saved as game partners of each other (only the two random account IDs and the time are stored; the player name is shown). For every player against whom you have finished a game the service also stores the overall record (number of wins and losses); it is shown to you with the partners and, mirrored, to the other account. With a saved partner you can start a new game without a code: the service keeps the invitation until it is accepted or declined, or until the game starts or is deleted. You can remove partners in the app at any time; when an account is deleted it also disappears from other people’s partner lists.

Blocking, reporting and bans. You can block fellow players (stored: the two account IDs; a block works in both directions and ends when you unblock or when an account is deleted) and report them. A report contains the reported account ID and player name, your account ID, a fixed reason (display name, behaviour, other) and the game ID, with no free text and no game content. I review reports to limit abuse (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); reports are deleted after 90 days. In case of abuse I can ban an account: the account is removed as in a deletion, and the email address is stored only as a check value (hash) so that it cannot sign up again; I can lift the ban.

Notifications (optional, off by default). If you turn on the notification “when it is your turn” under “Play online” in the app and Android grants the permission, the app requests a device identifier from Google Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google Ireland Limited / Google LLC) and sends it, together with the app language, to the online service. The service uses it to send a message (“Your turn” or an invitation) to your device via Firebase; the message contains only this notice and the game ID, no game content. As long as you do not turn the feature on, the app does not contact Firebase. The identifier is deleted when you turn the feature off, when you delete the account and when a device can no longer be reached; at most five devices are stored per account. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG), which you give by turning the feature on and withdraw at any time by turning it off.

What data is created.

  • Account: email address, ID, check values of the device keys, player name, time of creation and of last use.
  • Confirmation codes: only a check value of the code, the address, times and the number of attempts, for at most 24 hours after sending. Only your mailbox receives the code itself.
  • Games: invitation code, rules, seats with player names, the full game state (hands, Schlag, trump, trick, scores, stake, action counter, last actions, turn deadline) and the result. The service needs this to validate moves and to show every player their own view.
  • Weekly counter: for each account the service counts how many online games were started in the current calendar week (just one number per week, with no link to a game); counters of older weeks are deleted after two weeks at the latest. It is used to enforce the quota of the free use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Premium subscription: if you buy a subscription, the app sends the purchase token from Google Play to the service, which checks it through the Google Play interface (product, status, end of term, assigned account ID). When you buy, the app passes your random account ID to Google Play. Stored per account are the end of the premium term with its origin (“Google Play”) and an irreversible check value (hash) of the purchase token together with the account ID, so that a purchase is not assigned to several accounts; the check value is also created for expired or cancelled purchases. I do not receive payment data (card, bank details); Google handles the payment (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). When you open the online area, on every refresh of the list and via “Check purchases” the app sends the purchase tokens it holds again, so that renewals are recognised.
  • Presence: when the app reports whether you have the game open and the app is in the foreground, the service stores this only as a flag on the seat in the running game (solely for the turn-clock pause, see the terms of use); it disappears with the game.
  • Partners, invitations, block list and overall record: per account the IDs of the accounts concerned and the number of wins and losses against individual opponents (see above).
  • Reports: account IDs, the reported player’s name, fixed reason, game ID and time (see above).
  • Push identifier: only after you turn notifications on, together with the app language (see above).
  • Abuse protection: to limit requests (for example code requests) the service stores, for one day at most, an irreversible check value (hash) of the sender address and the date together with a counter, not the address itself; the counters are deleted daily (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • Technical connection data: when you connect, Cloudflare processes technically necessary data such as the IP address, time, requested address and browser or app identifier in its own logs to deliver and secure the service. I do not evaluate IP addresses myself and do not store them in plain text in the game database.

What other players see. Other people in the same game see your player name, the number of cards in your hand, the cards of the current trick, the Schlag, trump and scores, and your actions (calls, cards played, raising, looking, giving up). They do not see the cards in your hand. Outside shared games your name is not visible; there is no public player list and no search by name.

Purpose and legal basis. The processing serves to run online games including partners, invitations and the overall record (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) the handling of the premium subscription where you use it (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR), and the security, protection against abuse and stability of the service (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). That the app stores the account ID and key on your device and sends them to the service during online play is necessary for the service you requested (Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG).

Retention. Finished games are deleted 30 days after the game ends, games that were never started after seven days, and running games with a turn limit that nobody has looked at for 30 days after the deadline expired likewise, and running games without a turn limit in which nothing has happened for 30 days likewise. Expired codes are deleted after 24 hours at the latest, reports after 90 days, rate-limit counters daily. A ban (the check value of an email address) stays until I lift it. Push identifiers stay until you turn them off, until the account is deleted or until the device can no longer be reached. Partners, block list and overall record stay until one of the accounts involved is deleted or you remove the entry. Deletion runs once a day. The end of the premium term and the check value of the purchase token stay as long as the account exists. An account that has not been used for a year is deleted automatically together with its email address; otherwise it remains until you delete it (see below). A game always has four seats, at most two of them taken by a computer. Anyone who leaves a running game gives up their seat: a computer takes it over and the game continues. Without a turn limit nothing is played automatically; when a limit expires, the service acts for the seat itself (it makes a call, plays a legal card or gives up). After three missed limits in a row a computer takes over the seat as well.

Deleting your account. In the app under “Play online” → “Delete account”. This deletes the account, the email address, the device keys, the push identifiers, the premium status with the check value of the purchase token, your partners, invitations, block list and overall record (also in other people’s lists) and the link to your games; running games are forfeited. An existing subscription is not cancelled by this; you do that in Google Play. Reports already sent about you remain until their 90 days are up. You can also request deletion without the app: Delete account.

Location and transfers to third countries. The database is created with a location hint for Europe; however, Cloudflare processes requests through its global network, so processing in the USA and other countries is possible. According to its own statements Cloudflare relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses. You can get a copy of the safeguards on request by email; Cloudflare’s contractual terms are published on its website. Google (Play interface for checking purchases, Firebase) may also process data in the USA; according to its own statements Google likewise relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses and is itself responsible for payment handling and Play accounts.

Audience. The app is intended for people aged 18 and over.
